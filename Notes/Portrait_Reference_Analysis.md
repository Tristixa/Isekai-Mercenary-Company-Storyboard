## What the portrait demonstrates

### Selective drawn form

The image builds substantial volume through contours, angular cel-shadow shapes, painted tonal changes and selectively softened transitions. Hair, face and costume are visibly designed through drawing. Their clean appearance depends on controlled shape relationships and selective information, not realistic detail or surface texture.

The previous emphasis on photographic grounding and costume minimalism was too restrictive. The new master is more confidently stylized and more expressive in its designed armor/collar shapes, while reducing evenly distributed surface description.

### Face

The visible violet eye has a pronounced upper lid, designed lashes, clear iris and purposeful eyebrow. A lowered lid, partly concealed other eye, tapered jaw and restrained mouth create a composed, appraising impression. That emotional reading fits Tristitia; it is not a mandatory expression for the cast.

The nose and mouth are small and economically described. The nose bridge is largely implied. Warm skin transitions are soft but selective, and the cheek/forehead remain quiet. Freckles add identity without becoming general skin noise. A blanket ban on small noses/mouths or a requirement to fully model the bridge would work against this reference.

### Hair and silhouette

Broad crown and fringe masses break into curved, overlapping ribbons around the face and shoulders. Pale highlight ribbons and gray-lilac wedges articulate their turns. Several outer locks form deliberate gaps and loops; the silhouette is not sealed.

These locks have visible attachment, width, curvature and taper. This is the useful distinction from incidental threadlike flyaways. Clean outlines permit substantial flowing locks, openings and designed tips. A compact helmet silhouette would lose the master's movement.

The scarf's ragged contour and angular armor are intentional design shapes. Their presence is not itself evidence of bad edges. Pixel-level alpha defects must be assessed separately.

### Costume

The high collar frames the face. Stacked shoulder plates make one strong armored mass. Diagonal straps and scarf folds reinforce the torso turn. Pale fittings articulate the dark torso, and the sword guard forms a distinct lower focal shape.

There is considerable detail, but it belongs to larger structures and is separated by quiet panels. The correct lesson is organized detail and controlled contrast, not a strict count of buckles, plates or accessories. Functional construction supports the fantasy design without forcing generic utilitarian clothing.

Tristitia's fitted torso, neckline, asymmetry and sword are specific to this selected appearance. They must not become a uniform for every character.

### Materials and color

Skin is quieter and softer than the costume. Black cloth has large charcoal planes and selected deep fold/overlap shadows. The wine-red scarf has broad directional fold shapes. Metal has pale bevels, darker central planes and deep undersides, conveying reflectivity without dense scratches. Leather straps use muted browns and readable thickness.

This contradicts a universal matte treatment or uniformly sparse highlights. Broad metal highlights can be essential. Each material needs its own value and edge behavior.

Pale hair, warm skin, dark clothing, red scarf and pale metal form Tristitia's clear color hierarchy. For other characters, transfer the relationship of major light/dark/accent masses while keeping their individual palette.

### Framing, pose and game use

The source crops around the upper thighs, giving the head, shoulders, torso and hands much more canvas area than a head-to-boots image. A raised hand touches the scarf, a lower hand rests around the sword hilt, and the torso/head angles create a characterful pose.

In the supplied game screenshot, the portrait reads through the head and upper body: silver hair, visible eye, collar, scarf, shoulder armor and raised glove survive. The sword and lower costume disappear below the display without destroying identity. Fine freckles and fittings become secondary at this scale.

Therefore default dialogue portraits should allocate space to face and expressive upper-body design, adapting the full image to the actual crop. The screenshot is evidence of use and scale, not authorization to copy its background, UI or text.

## Rules replacing the earlier assumptions

- Strong anime-derived facial construction with selective modeling replaces realism-driven face instructions.

- Small simplified features are allowed when adult age, identity and expression read correctly.

- Designed flowing locks and openings replace a blanket prohibition on exterior hair shapes.

- Grouped detail and quiet areas replace an arbitrary accessory limit.

- Material-specific highlight behavior replaces a universal matte/sparse-highlight treatment.

- Dialogue-oriented framing replaces automatic full-body staging.

- Deliberate garment shape is judged separately from pixel-level edge quality.

- The actual master replaces rendering labels and alternate style presets as the visual authority.

This is style/reference approval. Future candidate acceptance and runtime integration remain separate decisions. Technical export artifacts are not part of the artistic style and must not be reproduced as features.
