Approved locks: the main river runs visibly through central Eurydica; the Old Bridge is the primary crossing and defining city landmark; the Civic Terrace remains simple and institutionally underdeveloped; and the district relationships and main south-to-north arrival sequence shown here are accepted as the basis for the next design phase. Exact wall shape, district proportions, secondary crossings and outbound-road geometry remain provisional.

## 1. Planning idea

Eurydica is organized around an old river crossing. The south road from Mosswood and the outskirts reaches a fortified arrival ward, continues through the market, and crosses the river into the older northern neighborhoods. Commercial and working districts spread along the water because transport, storage and processing depend on it. Residential streets climb the quieter eastern terrace.

This creates one legible arrival sequence:

**Outskirts → South Gate → Arrival Ward → Market Spine → Old Bridge → Civic Terrace**

The Company begins near the southern market edge, where adventurers, travelers, material sellers and outbound roads naturally overlap. It is strategically useful without already occupying the city's most prestigious address.

## 2. Whole-city schematic

Use the linked visual schematic as the single district-placement map. North is up.

- North bank: Old City Streets to the west, the small Civic Terrace at the bridge approach, Residential Quarter to the east, and Waterworks and Gardens along the northeastern river edge.
- South bank: Quays and Storeyards to the west, Market Spine at the bridge approach, and Service Lanes to the east.
- Southern arrival edge: Workshops to the southwest, Company Edge between Arrival Ward and Market Spine, and Arrival Ward at South Gate.
- Old Bridge joins the two banks. Cross-bank routes use Old Bridge until an additional crossing is explicitly designed.
- Mild terraces are expressed through foundations, retaining edges, courts and routes that accommodate the terrain. Exact elevations and parcel shapes are unresolved.

The visual distinguishes the approved principal route from provisional outward links and boundary geometry. Internal secondary routes are described below. District areas do not imply exact land allocation or relative institutional capacity.

## 3. District functions

### A. South Gate

- The first major Eurydica landmark seen from the outskirts.
- Provides guards, banners, controlled lighting and a view toward the denser city beyond.
- Uses older defensive masonry adapted with newer trade shelters and inspection space.
- Keeps the central road clear; waiting carts and deliveries move into side pockets.

### B. Arrival Ward

- Handles travelers before they enter the market proper.
- Contains modest inns, stable or pack-animal support, food sellers and cart services as supported by later lore.
- Feels busy and practical rather than ceremonially grand.
- Establishes Eurydica's maintained-but-improvised character immediately.

### C. Workshop Quarter

- Houses repair, basic processing, tools, containers and transport support.
- Connects directly to the market, quays and outbound road.
- Uses compacted sand/dirt courts and delivery lanes, wider doors, covered work edges and lower broad structures. The user's 2026-09-20 correction calls for sparse trees and neutral workshop roof colors; the working route toward the quays is unpaved.
- Gives Mae's profession a visible economic context without making her current arrangement prosperous.

### D. Quays and Storeyards

- Concentrates bulk goods beside the river rather than scattering crates across plazas.
- Uses retaining walls, stairs, ramps, hoists or other setting-appropriate handling structures.
- Forms Eurydica's rougher working edge while remaining maintained and organized.
- Water itself remains a later runtime or separate-layer concern; the concept establishes banks and infrastructure.

### E. Market Spine

- Eurydica's busiest everyday route, not one oversized square.
- Permanent shops form attached street walls; temporary stalls occupy designed bays and small widened nodes.
- The tavern sits near the arrival-side junction where travelers and independent adventurers naturally meet.
- The street culminates visually at the Old Bridge and layered northern roofline.

### F. Old Bridge — primary city landmark

- The city's primary spatial and symbolic landmark.
- Connects commerce and labor on the southern bank to the older northern neighborhoods.
- Shows accumulated construction: older piers or masonry, later repairs, current guard and maintenance fittings.
- Provides long views along the river and clear orientation without becoming a narrow navigation bottleneck.

### G. Old City Streets

- User-established history: this is Eurydica's original settlement, retained as the city expanded. Do not import Nefroburg's royal/castle history into Eurydica.
- Historic but maintained, including the streets: predominantly timber buildings with brick repairs/infill and stone foundations; inherited road alignments and repaired older paving rather than pristine uniform surfaces or ruined streets.
- Mae's user-described recommendation of Old City for its views is a design requirement: reveal river, bridge and city-roof views through street openings and modest overlooks. Exact viewpoints remain to be designed.
- Denser attached buildings and irregular parcels reveal Eurydica's age.
- Former walls, yards and passages have been absorbed into homes and businesses.
- Streets remain navigable but feel more enclosed than the southern market.
- Supplies the layered roofscape visible from the gate and bridge.

### H. Civic Terrace

- Remains a small public terrace rather than a developed administrative district.
- Contains only basic functions such as a toll or weights office, guard post, notice shelter and limited records room.
- Uses small reused buildings, patched paving and practical shelters; Old Bridge remains the primary landmark. Local roof curves, shaped windows, carved doors and color may be expressive even with limited institutional resources.
- Reveals an institutional gap: officials can maintain ordinary city order, but nobody coordinates adventurers, verifies dangerous work or assumes responsibility for expedition outcomes.
- Feels adequate for an older, smaller settlement and increasingly inadequate for present-day Eurydica.

### I. Residential Quarter

- Contains attached homes, small courts and quieter streets on the eastern rise.
- Shows household-specific planting, shade structures, repairs and small services.
- Provides daily-life evidence even when few NPCs are present.
- Connects to the civic terrace, market and eastern road without routing all traffic through private lanes.

### J. Waterworks and Gardens

- Uses the river/canal system for visible civic utility rather than decorative water alone.
- May include cistern access, controlled channels, public washing or planted embankments depending on later lore approval.
- Provides a softer green counterweight to the working quays.
- Must remain practical and structurally coherent rather than becoming a magical garden showcase.

### K. Service Lanes

- Supports food preparation, clinics, household trade and small local businesses.
- Creates intimate routes and shortcuts near the market while preserving a broad primary path.
- Uses owner-specific additions and threshold activity instead of generic façade repetition.

### L. Company Edge

- The Tier I Company begins in an adapted property between the Arrival Ward and Market Spine.
- It is close to outbound work, recruits, requests, the tavern and material trade.
- A yard or secondary structure allows later expansion to become visually obvious.
- The location is strategically promising but initially modest, supporting the “work becomes home” pillar.

## 4. Route hierarchy

### Primary route

South Gate → Arrival Ward → Market Spine → Old Bridge → Civic Terrace.

- Broad and readable under the gameplay camera.
- Carries arrivals, public movement and the strongest landmarks.
- Changes paving and enclosure gradually to reveal district transitions.

### Working route

South Gate/Arrival Ward → Workshops → Quays → Market storage edge.

- Keeps carts and bulk handling away from the main pedestrian center where possible.
- Surface direction: use sand/dirt for workshop courts and working routes toward the quays. South Gate → Arrival Ward → Market Spine AND Service Lanes streets and household courts are paved. Structural quay stone, foundations and thresholds remain appropriate. See the [approved south-bank layout](concepts/eurydica-south-bank-layout-approved.md) for accepted conceptual junctions; exact geometry remains unresolved.
- Explains material movement and the relationship between processing and trade.

### Residential route

Market Spine → Old Bridge → northern connections → Residential Quarter; Service Lanes connect locally to Market Spine on the south bank. East-road connections remain provisional.

- Quieter and more intimate, with domestic thresholds and small businesses.
- Avoids turning private lanes into the city's only route.

### Old-city loop

Old Bridge → Old City Streets → Civic Terrace → Residential Quarter → eastern crossing or bridge return.

- Makes the northern bank feel like a district network rather than a backdrop.
- Exact secondary crossing remains unresolved until the map scale is tested.

## 5. Chapter 1 playable focus

The first playable Eurydica slice does not need to expose the whole city.

Recommended Chapter 1 area:

- South Gate establishing approach.
- Arrival Ward transition.
- Southern Market Spine.
- Tavern.
- Company Edge.
- A framed view toward the Old Bridge and northern skyline.

Workshops, quays, residential lanes and the civic terrace can initially be visible, gated or reached through later district transitions. This preserves Nefroburg-scale fiction while keeping the opening exploration purposeful.

## 6. Landmark hierarchy

1. **Primary:** Old Bridge and the river corridor, dominant through composition and route importance.
   **Vertical orientation:** the inherited Old City watch-bell tower is the tallest architectural marker. Its exact shape remains open.
2. **Arrival:** South Gate silhouette.
3. **Commercial:** Market junction and tavern frontage.
4. **Player home:** Company banner/sign and adaptable yard.
5. **Working city:** Quay structures and workshop roofline.
6. **Daily life:** Residential terrace, gardens and service-lane additions.

No fountain should be asked to represent the entire city. Smaller wells, basins or civic ornaments may exist only where their use and placement make sense.

## 8. Open design questions

- Does Eurydica have a second major bridge, or only minor service crossings beyond the Old Bridge?
- Which eastern and western routes connect most strongly to the wider region?
- How formal should the walls remain: active defensive system, maintained historical boundary or partially absorbed old fortification?
