# Eurydica City Concept v0.1

Status: concept development only. No game scenes, geometry, collisions, lighting, markers or runtime assets are authorized for change by this document.

## 1. Current decisions

- Location: Eurydica.
- Environment type: large playable fantasy city composed of connected districts.
- Complete-city scale: comparable to Nefroburg from *Steambot Chronicles*—a substantial regional city, not a village, megacity or royal-capital sprawl.
- Approved geographic foundation: a river-crossing or canal-fed trade city with mild terraces.
- Approved river structure: the main river passes visibly through central Eurydica; the Old Bridge is the city's defining landmark and primary district crossing.
- Approved civic maturity: the Civic Terrace is small and institutionally underdeveloped. Eurydica has basic guards, tolls, notices and records, but no mature bureaucracy or organization responsible for adventurer work.
- Approved district structure: the 2026-09-19 whole-city schematic and its South Gate to Civic Terrace arrival sequence are the basis for silhouette, density and architectural development. Exact production geometry remains provisional.
- Approved silhouette and density layer: `eurydica-silhouette-density-study-v0.1.md` locks the city height hierarchy, roof rhythm, street-width hierarchy, inherited Old City watch-bell tower and regional architectural traits as of 2026-09-20.
- Current development layer: `eurydica-facade-material-language-v0.1.md` proposes the shared facade grammar, city palette, district variation and controlled lived-in treatment for approval.
- Current task: establish the city concept before generating a concept image or changing the game.
- Current prototype: evidence for 3D feasibility and gameplay framing, not a layout or final art lock.
- Steambot references: scale, infrastructure, urban layering, functional architecture, visible ownership and daily life.
- The current orthographic three-quarter presentation remains useful project context, but the exact concept-image and production camera will be selected before image generation.
- Final rendering family is unresolved. 3D-anime-rendered 2D JRPG, Unicorn Overlord background style and PawPop 2 background style remain separate later choices.

## 2. Canon and project constraints already established

- Eurydica is close to dangerous outskirts and Mosswood; the story places the outskirts about ten minutes from the city gate.
- The city has guards, banners, warm occupied windows, a tavern, a material market and the Company's first base.
- Adventurers currently obtain work independently, through informal groups or through nobles. There is no established Adventurer's Guild.
- Reliable processing, buyers and accountability are missing. The new Company exists because this institutional gap creates practical problems for adventurers and residents.
- Existing civic offices cover ordinary city needs only. They do not recruit adventurers, verify requests, coordinate expeditions, process monster materials or take responsibility for failed work.
- Chapter 1 ends by letting the player explore Eurydica and return to the Company before 14:00.
- The project roadmap favors a hybrid world: connected Eurydica districts with separate expedition-region scenes.
- Eurydica must later respond visibly to Company work through business supply, repairs, patrols, routes, meals, residents and request outcomes.

## 3. Core city premise

**Eurydica is a maintained but strained regional trade city at the edge of increasingly unsafe country.**

It is large enough to contain several neighborhoods, markets, workshops, storeyards and old infrastructure, but its civic organization has not developed at the same pace as the city. Guards protect gates and officials handle basic tolls, notices, disputes and records; those functions remain modest and fragmented. Commerce exists; coordination does not. Adventurers, processors, merchants and residents depend on one another without a trustworthy institution joining them.

This makes the city the correct birthplace for the Company. The Company should initially look small relative to Eurydica, then become more visible as it earns trust and changes local routines.

The desired emotional combination is:

- **Arrival and safety:** the walls, lights and occupied buildings contrast with the dangerous outskirts.
- **Familiarity:** the city is warm, legible and maintained rather than oppressive.
- **Accumulated life:** repairs, extensions, reused structures and district routines reveal age.
- **Unfinished potential:** empty institutional space explains why the Company can matter.
- **Other-world identity:** recognizable urban functions are expressed through Eurydica's own coherent fantasy construction language.

## 4. Scale model

Nefroburg defines the scale of the complete city, not the amount that must be loaded or fully explorable at once.

Eurydica should imply:

- Multiple connected districts rather than a single market square.
- A recognizable bridge, gate and layered roofline visible from the outskirts.
- At least one major piece of infrastructure that shaped urban growth.
- Dense central street walls with smaller courts and service lanes behind them.
- A clear contrast between formal public areas, commercial frontage, residential life and working/logistics edges.
- Enough population and economic activity that taverns, markets, processing work, guards, nobles and independent adventurers all plausibly coexist.

The recommended game structure is a sequence of connected district scenes or streamed district cells. Distant roofs, towers, walls and traffic routes can establish the full scale without making every building enterable.

## 5. Approved geographic anchor

The approved foundation is **a river-crossing or canal-fed trade city with mild terraces**.

The main river visibly divides central Eurydica. The Old Bridge carries the principal gate-to-civic route across it and serves as the city's strongest orientation landmark. Smaller canals may support drainage, waterworks or working quays later, but they do not replace the main river as the organizing feature.

Why it fits:

- It gives Eurydica a reason to exist as a market and transport center.
- A bridge, retaining wall, quay, drainage channel or raised road can visibly shape the districts.
- It supports a memorable approach from the outskirts and a readable bridge-and-roofline silhouette.
- It creates natural distinctions between market, residential and logistics areas without relying on arbitrary decoration.
- It transfers Nefroburg's infrastructure-led credibility without copying its industrial period.

The district diagram and later images must preserve this geographic logic. Exact river width, minor canal branches, secondary crossings and the terrace profile remain later design decisions.

## 6. Proposed city anatomy

### A. South Gate and Arrival Road

Narrative job: first proof of safety and scale after the outskirts.

- Guarded gate, layered wall construction and visible occupied skyline beyond.
- A broad entry route for pedestrians, pack animals and carts.
- Inspection, waiting and unloading pockets kept beside—not in—the main route.
- Older defensive fabric visibly adapted for ordinary trade.

### B. Market Spine

Narrative job: introduce Eurydica's economy and its imperfect distribution network.

- Permanent shops and attached upper residences form the street wall.
- Temporary stalls occupy planned edge bays instead of floating in a featureless plaza.
- Storage, deliveries and closing routines are visible behind or beside sellers.
- The tavern sits on this route because travelers, workers and independent adventurers naturally meet here.

### C. Civic Crossing

Narrative job: orient the player while showing that Eurydica's physical growth exceeds its civic development.

- A bridge, old crossing, raised junction or other geographic response becomes the primary landmark.
- A small toll office, guard post, notice shelter or record house handles basic public needs in reused or modest buildings.
- The ground remains broad enough for circulation, gatherings and story staging.
- The space should be asymmetrical and historically layered rather than a perfect decorative plaza.
- Nothing here resembles a developed adventurer institution. Requests still circulate through private contacts, taverns, merchants and nobles.

### D. Workshop and Storeyard Quarter

Narrative job: make material processing and logistics part of the visible city economy.

- Durable ground, loading doors, covered work edges and repair yards.
- Buildings are lower, broader and more practical than civic or residential structures.
- Stored goods appear at points of use and transfer, not as scattered fantasy clutter.
- The district shows why Mae's processing skills have economic value.

### E. Residential and Service Lanes

Narrative job: show that Eurydica is a home, not only a quest hub.

- Narrower secondary routes, small courts, household additions and planted thresholds.
- Clinics, food sellers and everyday services are visually distinct from houses.
- Laundry structures, herb boxes, rain shelters and repairs appear in controlled edge clusters.
- Major movement routes stay readable even when side spaces feel intimate.

### F. Company Edge

Narrative job: make the Tier I Company modest, useful and capable of growth.

- The initial base occupies an adapted building or compound near commerce and outbound routes, not the city's grandest site.
- The headquarters should visibly connect to market supply, adventurer arrivals and the road toward expedition regions.
- Expansion opportunities remain legible in adjacent yard, storage and workshop space.
- Company colors and signage begin as restrained owner-specific additions rather than citywide authority.

## 7. Urban design rules

### City before props

Design in this order:

1. Geography and city edge.
2. Main infrastructure and approach.
3. District locations and connections.
4. Landmark hierarchy and skyline.
5. Street widths, public nodes and service routes.
6. Building families and ownership.
7. Props, wear, vegetation and daily-life evidence.

Do not begin by placing benches, planters, barrels and stalls into an empty paved field.

### Route hierarchy

- Primary routes: wide, continuous and immediately readable at gameplay scale.
- Secondary streets: connect districts and reveal attached building fronts.
- Service lanes: imply storage, deliveries and household access without becoming mandatory navigation mazes.
- Public nodes: occur where routes and institutions meet; they are not arbitrary empty squares.

### Building hierarchy

Use several related families:

- Bridge, gate and modest municipal/service structures.
- Attached commercial buildings with living space above.
- Narrow residences and courtyard homes.
- Workshops, warehouses and service sheds.
- Tavern and other business-specific façades.
- Later additions, lean-tos, bridges and reused wall fragments.

Shared materials create regional coherence. Different footprints, heights, rooflines, thresholds and adaptations communicate function.

### Open space

Playable districts should preserve approximately 80% connected, usable ground and 20% scenery occupancy. This does not mean one huge empty plaza.

Open ground can show:

- Worn travel bands.
- Repaired paving.
- Drainage routes.
- Loading or market zones.
- Cleaner swept thresholds.
- Quiet material changes between districts.

Concentrate object clusters along edges so the center remains navigable.

## 8. Lived-in construction requirements

Every district must show all three layers.

### Past

- Reused foundations or wall sections.
- Restrained repaired paving and replacement roof materials.
- Filled openings, practical extensions or buildings absorbed into older infrastructure.

### Present owner

- Business-specific frontage and storage.
- Household-specific shutters, planting and additions.
- Basic civic maintenance concentrated on gates, bridge access and principal routes rather than polished administration.
- The Company's modest new colors, sign and work adaptations.

### Daily routine

- Cart and pedestrian wear on actual routes.
- Loading, waiting and work-contact areas.
- Swept versus less-used ground.
- Furniture and goods stored where they are used.
- Limited NPC schedules later, only where they support story or gameplay.

Age must come from use and change, not random grime, broken construction or blanket procedural distress.

## 9. Proposed fantasy design vocabulary — pending approval

Retain familiar materials but give Eurydica a small repeatable regional language:

- Warm pale stone and maintained plaster as the dominant mass.
- Dark structural timber and iron used according to believable support and attachment.
- Terracotta roofs mixed with weathered blue-green metal or glazed ceramic on important public or water-management elements.
- Deep navy and restrained gold for civic wayfinding and, later, Company identity.
- One recognizable Eurydican arch, roof-ridge or bracket profile repeated across different building types.
- Water control, drainage or rain handling treated as designed architecture rather than hidden utility if the river/canal geography is approved.

This vocabulary should create fantasy identity through construction. Avoid solving fantasy with universal crystals, glowing runes, excessive spires or generic medieval clutter.

## 10. Palette and atmosphere

- Dominant: warm pale stone, aged cream plaster, muted road stone.
- Secondary: dark timber, terracotta, weathered blue-green and vegetation greens.
- Accents: navy, restrained gold, business cloth colors and small ceramic color groups.
- Day state: clear morning with enough value contrast to distinguish buildings from playable ground.
- Night state: safety is communicated by selective occupied windows and runtime lighting, not by every façade glowing.

The city should feel colorful and maintained without looking freshly unpacked or uniformly polished.

## 11. Reference use

The retained Nefroburg images are stored under `references/steambot-chronicles-nefroburg/`.

- City overview: complete-city scale, surrounding geography, civic axis and district hierarchy.
- Canal and bridge: infrastructure-driven composition, urban depth and mixed building heights.
- Station façade: function-specific architecture, structural materials, maintenance and ownership.

They are not camera references and do not authorize copying Nefroburg's street plan, buildings, machinery, historical period, haze or lighting filter.

## 12. Concept production sequence

Before a polished Eurydica image, produce and approve:

1. **City premise and geography:** approved—Nefroburg-scale regional trade city organized around a river/canal crossing and mild terraces.
2. **District diagram:** simple top-down plan showing gate, main crossing, market, civic node, workshops, residences, Company and routes.
3. **Silhouette study:** skyline and building-height families viewed from the outskirts/gate approach.
4. **Architecture and material sheet:** six to ten representative structures/details showing the shared fantasy vocabulary and controlled age.
5. **Gameplay-district blockout:** verify route widths, camera, character scale, UI clearance and open-space balance.
6. **Hero concept image:** portray the approved city identity and one representative district.
7. **Production breakdown:** only after approval, define modular buildings, ground, props, lighting, water and runtime integration needs.
