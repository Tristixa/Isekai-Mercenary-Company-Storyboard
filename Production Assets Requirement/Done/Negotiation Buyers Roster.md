# Negotiation Buyers Roster

Written 2026-09-28. These are the buyers Valerie brings to the **Counter-offer** working session (GDD 5a.2a). Each morning Valerie posts one **negotiation order**: one of these buyers wants goods drawn from the Guild's current storage. The Commander haggles over 3–5 rounds, reading the buyer's face for the **tell**.

- **Eurydica** (Chapters 1–5) has one buyer: the Travelling Merchant.
- **The Frontier** (Chapter 6 onward) has four senior traders from rival merchant factions, two men and two women, each with a different persona.

Names and designs below are **proposals** until the owner approves them. Game rules (hidden ceiling, patience, tactics, grading) live in the GDD; this file owns the **look, persona and asset list**. If the two disagree about a number, the GDD wins.

## 1. Rules for every buyer

- **The roster's general rules apply:** adult and appealing, distinct silhouettes, never sharing an officer's signature, sprites without mouths.
- **The face is the gameplay.** In this minigame the portrait expression IS the tell, so every buyer needs the full negotiation expression set below. The expressions must read clearly apart even at a small portrait size.
- **Personas bend the tells.** An honest buyer's expression matches how close your price is to their hidden ceiling. The Frontier's harder buyers hide or fake it. Each entry below says how their tells behave, so the expressions can be drawn to support it (for example, a poker face that barely changes).

## 2. Assets each buyer needs

| Asset | Every buyer |
|---|---|
| Portrait, locked style (`Portrait Styles/`), **8 negotiation expressions** (list below) | Yes |
| Gemini sprite sheet, 4-direction idle (they stand at the Trading Post counter or their stall) | Yes |
| Walk video | No (they don't walk) |
| Work or greet video | Optional: one short greet loop |
| Key colour | Green #00FF00, unless the character wears green; then magenta #FF00FF |

**The 8 negotiation expressions:**

| Expression | Meaning in the minigame |
|---|---|
| **Neutral** | Default, listening; also the opening look |
| **Pleased** | Your price is comfortably under their ceiling (the "warm" tell) |
| **Considering** | Close to their ceiling: thinking it over (hand to chin, eyes aside) |
| **Doubtful** | A little over the ceiling, or an unconvincing tactic |
| **Irritated** | Well over the ceiling, or a tactic that offends them |
| **Surprised** | Reacts to a strong tactic (Show quality with Pristine goods, a generous bundle) |
| **Deal** | Satisfied, closing the deal (a handshake smile) |
| **Walk-away** | Patience gone: dismissive, turning away |

## 4. The Frontier

Materials from the Frontier are **big business**. The land is high-risk and high-opportunity, and every great trading power wants a share. So the Frontier buyers aren't local shopkeepers. Each one is a senior dealmaker for one of **four rival merchant factions**, sent to buy from the Guild that brings back what nobody else can. Rivalry between the factions is where their personas come from, and later story hooks can grow out of it.

**The four factions (proposed):**

| Faction | What it is | What it wants from the Frontier |
|---|---|---|
| **The Frontier Exchange** | A speculative trading house that finances risky expeditions and resells Frontier goods at a premium | Anything new and daring; it prizes the Guild's boldest hauls |
| **The Iron Ledger Consortium** | A banking and merchant consortium that underwrites ventures and supplies the southern workshops | Bulk materials of reliable quality, bought by contract |
| **House Chamerolles** | An old noble merchant house selling Frontier rarities to the aristocracy | Rare, elite and Pristine parts: trophies and curiosities |
| **The Meridian Charter Company** | A chartered company holding the only supply road between the coast and the Frontier | Useful goods, paid in supplies, equipment and shipping credit (barter) |

## 5. Adding buyers later

New buyers use this format. For every new region or base, give each buyer a persona that bends the tells in a new way, so the minigame keeps teaching something. Keep the roster small (4–5 per base) so each face becomes familiar.
