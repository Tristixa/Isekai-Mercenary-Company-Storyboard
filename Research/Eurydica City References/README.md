# Eurydica city references

## THE LAYOUT RULE (owner, 2026-10-01; from f01–f04): tight means clustered, not crowded

The owner's own references are `f01 Top Down`, `f02 bird view`, `f03 illustrated` (the best example) and `f04 walled city`.
"Tight" means buildings are **grouped close to each other**, not isolated. There is still negative space for greenery in the city.

1. **Buildings come in clusters** of 3–8 that touch or nearly touch (0–2 m apart): a shop row, a courtyard of houses, a workshop yard. Almost no building stands alone, except deliberate landmarks.
2. **The negative space sits *between* clusters, never between individual buildings.** Greenery, trees, gardens and fields fill the gaps between groups; there's no lawn inside a group. (Plan v5's failure: an even gap around every building, so each house sits alone on a patch of grass.)
3. **Few, obvious roads** (f03): one main road from the gate through the town, with a few clear branches and stairs. Road edges are where clusters stop.
4. **Wayfinding hierarchy: district → cluster → building.** The player finds "the shop row by the red tree" first, then the shop. Each cluster has one purpose and one look.
5. **Level changes, walls and gates mark districts** (f03's terraces and stairs, f01's wall). It's denser inside the walls; farmland and scattered buildings are outside.
6. **Landmarks punctuate the clusters:** the red tree, the clock tower, the gate, the tavern.

Not wanted: uniform shrinking or wall-to-wall packing everywhere (plan v6 draft, rejected).

## Rules to take from them (and check v6 against)

1. **Inside the walls is built up, and green is outside.** Green inside only as single trees, planters and one or two small squares.
2. **Street walls are continuous.** Houses share walls and front the street directly, with no front gardens and no setbacks.
3. **Streets are narrow and bend,** and a street should end in a **view-closer**: a gate tower, a church or the clock tower.
4. **Small plazas where streets meet** (the Plönlein fork, market squares) do the job lawns were doing: breathing room, but paved.
5. **Roofs overlap and step.** From above, the town reads as one mass of roofs with streets cut through it.
6. **Colour comes from the fronts:** painted plaster, timber, shutters, window boxes and hanging signs.

## How to use them

- **If v6 still feels like a village:** a v7 brief can cite these, especially Rothenburg's street fronts and fork plaza, and Český Krumlov's river-bend density.
- **For painting:** these photos can be attached as **layout or composition references only**, never as style (the painting manner is fixed by the approved facades and the Hylaea set).
