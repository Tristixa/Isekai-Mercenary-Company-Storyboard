## Main finding

Attention changes even when the characters remain in the same place. The scene directs us toward speaking, listening, withholding, noticing, and deciding. It does not require constant walking or a large gesture on every line.

## Observations and interpretation

### Position establishes the relationship

At 00:16–00:38, the Traveler and Paimon approach a seated Dainsleif who largely faces away from them. Their positions make his lack of engagement visible before Paimon comments on it.

At 00:39–00:40, a close view isolates his turn. At approximately 00:41, the composition widens for the invitation to sit. Interpretation: the change of attention is an event, and the closer framing makes a small physical response consequential.

IMC implication: Elsie can be occupied with a pack before Vanilla arrives. Distinguish noticing him from giving him her full attention for the interview.

### Camera attention need not follow the speaking character

At 03:12, Dainsleif explains his final question. Around 03:16–03:20, the Traveler is framed while his question remains displayed and response options appear. The image attends to the person deciding.

At 11:04–11:12, Dainsleif's unfinished sentence remains on screen while the view shifts to the Traveler reacting and turning. Her attention moves away from the exchange before the choice text explains the interruption.

IMC implication: Vanilla can be visibly thinking, following a gesture, or noticing something while an officer speaks. Select the subject whose response changes the scene rather than switching camera solely by speaker name.

### Framing changes give selected lines emphasis

At 02:22–02:26, Dainsleif's question uses an ordinary conversational view. At 02:27–02:28, his evasive reply has a much tighter face shot. At 02:29, the view returns to the table and listeners.

Interpretation: the tighter view briefly intensifies his unsettling knowledge; the wider view restores the social exchange. Do not infer that all closer shots serve this same purpose.

IMC implication: preserve comfortable shared views for ordinary explanations. Reserve closer framing for meaningful scrutiny, hesitation, praise, or withheld answers when the visuals can support it.

### Gesture scale differs by character

Paimon frequently uses open arms, changing hand positions, head tilts, and gestures near her face. At 01:20–01:24, her pose changes while her uncertain thought continues.

Dainsleif is relatively contained. Around 02:40, a hand near his face makes his consideration of a response visible. The Traveler commonly shares Paimon's framing with less emphatic physical performance.

IMC implication: vary physical timing and intensity by character. Tristitia's precision, Mae's unhurried practical activity, Elsie's social ease and professional attention, and Vanilla's curiosity should differ. These are working staging interpretations, not new canonical animation requirements. Avoid one repeated gesture becoming a substitute for personality.

### Shared frames preserve relationships

The tavern conversation often retains a listener's head or shoulders in the foreground while the other speaker is seen across the table. Props and furniture reinforce shared space.

At 10:32–10:56, talk turns toward having a home. Paimon addresses the Traveler about their future, and the composition favors that pair as their attention turns toward one another. The active relationship changes without anyone entering or leaving.

IMC implication: in a three-person scene, an introduction can begin as a group composition, develop into an Elsie–Vanilla exchange, and include Tristitia again when her response matters. She still needs a believable listening state while outside the visual emphasis.

### Environment becomes a subject when needed

Around 05:20, the temple entrance occupies substantial frame space while discussed. Around 07:20, the phenomenon above the fire is visible with the characters. Around 11:32, the view shows the cliff associated with the destination.

IMC implication: show relevant objects and places at the moment they help the player understand. The ledger, damaged strap, office doorway, and dormitory already provide usable subjects. Not every environmental object needs handling or an insert shot; tavern props often provide depth without becoming actions.

### Player choices require waiting and recovery states

The recording shows held text and choice screens alongside character poses and framing. Exact hold length can depend on the recorded player's input.

Implementation recommendation for IMC: distinguish one-time actions from stable waiting poses. Elsie withdrawing her hand or returning a salute should settle into a pose that can wait for input. Complete each branch's physical action before shared dialogue resumes: Vanilla stands after kneeling, hands lower after salutes, and prop ownership remains consistent.

## Candidate manuscript notation

At meaningful beats, specify: dialogue trigger, camera framing, actor action, listener reaction, and how the action ends or waits for player input. A setup may cover several lines; do not mechanically add a cut or gesture to each line.

## Limits and comparison questions

- Do not infer Genshin's internal animation tools, shot automation, rig costs, or production workflow from final footage.
- The study supports attention-driven staging. It does not establish a mandatory number of shots, gestures, or seconds per line.
- IMC's presentation and animation capabilities will determine usable framing. Small sprites and limited poses may favor shared composition, orientation, object interaction, and modest reframing over face close-ups.
- Future comparison with Steambot Chronicles should examine how much can be achieved with persistent conversation views, simple facing changes, player choices, selective movement, and clear returns to gameplay.
- No manuscript, character sheet, bible, or skill was changed as part of this study.
