# Atmosphere reference: Kingdoms of Amalur: Reckoning

The owner, 2026-09-30: "for fantasy feel of the town I think the game Kingdoms of Amalur have the best fantasy atmosphere." These six screenshots are the **atmosphere** reference for Eurydica and later towns. They set mood, light and dressing, not layout, camera or asset style. FGC keeps its HD-2D camera, painted facades and pixel sprites, and nothing from Amalur is copied.

## What we take (and how it maps to FGC)

| Amalur trait | In FGC |
|---|---|
| Warm, saturated sunlight with strong light–shadow contrast | The day grade: warmer highlights, richer greens and reds, deeper cool shadows (post stack) |
| Blue **aerial haze**: distance softens toward the sky colour | Depth fog tinted to the sky, plus the tilt-shift; the far city and hills fade to blue |
| **Autumn-colour trees** (red, pink, orange) as accents among lush green | Eurydica's iconic **big red tree** at the red-tree court, and a few warm-foliage trees mixed into the greenery |
| **Warm lanterns** that glow even by day; lit windows at dusk | Lamp posts and door lanterns with a soft emissive glow and bloom; evening window glow |
| **Chimney smoke** rising from roofs | Light smoke particles from the roof-kit chimneys |
| **Lived-in dressing**: banners, cloth awnings, barrels, flowers, mossy stones, fences | Dense prop clusters (plan v5), flower boxes, cloth and banners; mossy stone edges (ground cutouts) |
| Storybook architecture: swept roofs, timber framing, a landmark on the skyline | Already our facades and curved roofs; the 28 m clock tower as Eurydica's skyline landmark |
| Lush, vivid grass with dappled light | The approved ground tiles plus edge cutouts; soft shadows |

## How Amalur's look was made (research, 2026-09-30)

From the art director **Tim Coman** (Big Huge Games) and the executive art director **Todd McFarlane**:

- **Designed, not realistic.** Coman: "Everything is designed. Like, the ground is designed. The trees are designed… almost approaching like a Pixar film." That matches FGC's painted, HD-2D approach: every tile, tree and facade is a designed painting, not a photo texture.
- **Saturated on purpose.** They deliberately rejected the desaturated "bleach bypass" look of the time.
- **Colour comes from people, not nature.** McFarlane: "nature's colors aren't really vibrant" apart from the sky and flowers, so the team added vibrancy through **clothing, buildings and architecture**. They avoided the trap of "we want to be realistic, so we're just going to put a lot of brown in there."
- **Each zone has its own palette.** Reviewers note the palette shifts between zones, and the Fae areas glow.
- **Readability first.** McFarlane: "It's not right if the consumer is confused at any moment."
- **Towns are logical.** Coman's teams worked out each city's water supply, transport and economy so the settlements feel functional. The Eurydica plan already does this (waterworks, quays, market spine).
- **Soft over saturated.** Re-Reckoning reviewers describe a "soft muted filter in the lighting" over the rich colour, a classic high-fantasy haze. That's our warm grade plus blue haze, kept gentle.

## What this adds for Eurydica

1. **Man-made colour:** coloured awnings and cloth on the market stalls and shops, banners, flower boxes and planters, painted doors and shutters. The ground and plants stay natural. The phase-1 facade brief (v3) already asks for awnings, lanterns and flower boxes.
2. **District palettes:** the market is warm and colourful (green roofs, bright cloth); the civic terrace is stone and pale with the green clock-tower cap; the Service Lanes are warm red roofs with homely clutter; the quays and workshops are brown timber with piled goods. This follows the plan's roof colour rule.
3. **Flowers as the natural accent:** flower beds and window boxes, especially around the red-tree court and the homes.
4. **Keep it readable:** the Commander, NPCs and doors must always stand out, and saturation never beats readability (FGC_08's readability rule).

## Fantasy plants (the owner's correction, 2026-09-30: "not flowers but plants, fantasy plants")

Amalur's world is dotted with **alchemical reagent plants** that you can harvest, and they read clearly as magical: "all glowy and ready to be picked." Examples from the game's own descriptions:
- **Embereyes:** "seeds like smoldering stones radiate an intense heat from this pungent plant";
- **Eel Petal:** its juice "tingles with the essence of Lightning";
- **Cripplespore Caps:** deadly fungus caps;
- **Essence of Fate:** "a strange tangle of glowing blue thread";
- also Bloodroot, Sky Blossom, Scarwood Bark, Leechwood Bark, Seaflax and Scarlet Flowstone.

The Fae forest Dalentarth is described as "lush greens… intoxicating flowers… sky-scraping trees… vivacious, neon colors."

**For FGC: our own plants, never Amalur's names or designs.** A small fantasy flora for Eurydica, used as **set dressing** in town. In the Frontier they can become real consumable ingredients for the Research Department (`Game Design/Consumables and Research Proposal.md`). Each plant has a simple, readable silhouette and a single magical accent (a soft glow, an odd colour or an unusual shape), painted in the approved Hylaea manner.

| Plant (working name) | Look | Where in Eurydica |
|---|---|---|
| **Emberseed** | a low bush whose seed pods glow warm orange like coals | kitchen and herb gardens, the tavern's planters |
| **Stormfern** | a fern with blue-violet tips that shimmer faintly | shady lanes, the waterworks, beside walls |
| **Lantern cap** | a cluster of mushrooms with softly glowing pale gold caps | damp corners, under the big red tree, the quays |
| **Silverleaf herb** | an upright herb with silver-white leaves and a pale shine | the clinic's planters, allotment beds |
| **Crimson creeper** | a creeping vine with deep red leaves (it echoes the red tree) | walls, fences, arches |
| **Skybell** | a tall stalk hung with small bell-shaped sky-blue blossoms | beside doors, window boxes, the civic terrace |
| **Glassreed** | slender, faintly translucent reeds with dew-bead tips | the river bank, the waterworks, water troughs |

The **glows are engine-owned** (the no-baked-light rule): the paint keeps the plant unlit, and the game adds a small emissive or soft light on the marked glowing parts, more visible at dusk and night. It fits the lamp and night pass.
