# Tristitia Fidei

## Character authority

This sheet is the authority for Tristitia's characterization. Draft dialogue and scene notes should be revised to fit this sheet rather than being allowed to redefine her accidentally.

## Role

- Supporting character and one of the Company's founding officers.
- Chapter 1: Active adventurer who takes public hunts and other work involving dangerous people.
- Chapter 2 onward: Company Vice.

As Company Vice, Tristitia turns field experience into judgment, policy, and coordination. She is the officer who understands what the Company's decisions mean once people leave the safety of the headquarters.

## Physical Description

- Age: Mid 20s.
- Height: Approximately 168 cm.
- Build: Slim but toned.
- Distinguishing features: Silver hair, violet eyes, and beauty marks around her lips and cheek.

## Core Identity

Tristitia is a composed, capable adventurer whose skills have often been used to end threats rather than build anything lasting. She wants a legitimate road where those abilities can protect, organize, and guide people instead of merely ending them.

She is not seeking instant forgiveness or a dramatic confession. Her desire is more practical and more difficult: to make her competence useful without allowing violence to remain the only thing that gives her value.

Her central contradiction is:

> She wants a life beyond killing, but killing is the skill at which she is most competent.

She carries herself with the control and confidence of someone raised around status, command, or both. Whether she is actually noble, was once noble, or merely learned the manner remains undisclosed during the early story. The player may suspect, but the character should not confirm it casually.

## Personality

### Surface traits

- Regal and self-possessed.
- Sassy through precision rather than constant joking.
- Cool-headed under pressure.
- Restrained and difficult to read.
- Comfortable giving instructions and setting boundaries.

### Strengths

- Excellent judgment in dangerous situations.
- Observant and difficult to surprise.
- Competent with field work, pursuit, and threat assessment.
- Protects people indirectly without demanding gratitude.
- Respects capable people and their autonomy.
- Can make hard decisions without panicking.

### Weaknesses

- Emotionally guarded and easily mistaken for uncaring.
- Has difficulty asking for help or accepting direct concern.
- Can treat violence as the familiar solution to a complicated problem.
- Tends to measure her worth by usefulness and competence.
- May accept morally gray work too readily because she is accustomed to it.
- Her indirect support can become dismissive when she is tired or afraid.

## Work and Background

Tristitia's work can include several kinds of dangerous human-targeted jobs:

- Public bounty and bandit hunts.
- Private assassination contracts.
- Jobs connected to noble, political, or factional interests.

The exact history and balance of those jobs can be revealed gradually. They should not all be explained in Chapter 1. In Chapter 1, her active job is a public hunt for bandits in the Eurydica outskirts.

Her past should be understood through what she knows, what she avoids saying, and how naturally she handles danger. The story does not need to declare her entire history before the player has a reason to care about it.

## Goals and Motivation

### Immediate goal

Find a new road out of the killings without pretending that her past skills and choices never existed.

### Long-term direction

Help build a Company that can prepare people, direct them responsibly, and bring them home. As Company Vice, she should become the person who turns ideals into workable decisions in the field.

### Emotional need

Learn that she can be valuable because she protects and organizes people, not only because she is good at removing threats.

## Voice and Dialogue Rules

- Use concise, controlled sentences.
- Her speech is polished but not excessively ornate.
- She often answers the practical question beneath what was said.
- Praise is rare, measured, and therefore meaningful.
- Her humor comes from dry precision and restrained disbelief.
- She may leave a deliberate silence after revealing more than intended.
- She does not explain her feelings in long speeches unless a major emotional threshold has been reached.
- Her care is usually expressed through instructions, positioning, warnings, or quietly solving a problem.

Tristitia is not a quip machine. She should not sound playful, flirtatious, or theatrically cruel in every exchange. Her coolness comes from emotional restraint and disciplined self-control.

Example:

Adventurer: “That room looks heavily guarded. I'm going in.”

Tristitia: “Then go, if you think you can do it.”

If the adventurer succeeds, she may reveal that she was more impressed than she first appeared.

Tristitia: “Impressive work as usual, Adventurer.”

Tristitia: “...”

The silence may suggest embarrassment, a withheld thought, or simple consideration. The story should not always explain it.

## Chapter 1 Behavior

- She wakes the Player because leaving an unconscious stranger exposed would be impractical, not because she immediately feels responsible for them.
- She gives the Player a safe place to wait and expects the instruction to be followed.
- She leaves to complete the bandit hunt rather than abandoning her current work for the Player.
- When she returns, she notices whether the Player respected her instructions.
- She escorts the Player toward Eurydica because it is the responsible practical choice.
- The Company idea catches her attention because it may offer a legitimate road out of her current work.
- Her commitment should be cautious and restrained, but emotionally significant.

## Relationships

### Steady Mae

Mae and Tristitia share long-earned, functional trust. Mae handles the aftermath of Tristitia's work, including the processing and practical consequences that Tristitia does not want to discuss.

Tristitia trusts Mae with the things she does not say aloud. Mae is one of the few people who can question her without making Tristitia feel examined or pitied. Their relationship is closer to old companionship than open sentimentality.

### The Player / Commander

At first, Tristitia treats the Player as an unknown variable and possible liability. Trust begins when the Player follows instructions, keeps promises, and demonstrates that their ideas can survive contact with practical problems.

She should not treat the Player as a chosen savior. She sees the Player as someone who may provide the structure needed to turn a dangerous occupation into a functioning Company.

## Core Feeling

> “I trust you to handle yourself. I am still watching.”
