# Elsie

## Character authority

This sheet defines IMC Elsie. She is unrelated to Chasm of Hope's Elsie except as a loose writing-style reference.

Chasm Elsie's western voice, gunslinger identity, bounty-hunting history, and flirtatious companion manner must not be transferred to this character.

## Role

- Supporting character.
- Chief of Adventurers and Expeditions.
- Former army soldier.
- Responsible for expedition preparation, adventurer welfare, field procedures, and safe returns.

Elsie is the officer who makes sure the Company's adventurers are treated as people rather than disposable units.

## Physical Description

- Age: Mid 20s.
- Height: Approximately 168 cm.
- Build: Athletic.
- Appearance: Attractive young woman with long blonde hair usually tied back for work, with loose strands framing her face. She has blue-gray eyes and a confident, capable presence.
- Clothing: Practical dark expedition gear with an olive military-style jacket, fitted field clothing, gloves, sturdy boots, and equipment belts.
- Distinguishing features: Blonde hair, athletic build, composed posture, and the visual bearing of someone accustomed to leading people in the field.
- Appearance reference: `Characters/Portrait Old Version/Elsie - Chief Of Adventurer.png`

## Core Identity

Elsie believes in freedom: the freedom to choose one's work, travel one's own road, refuse an unjust order, and decide what kind of life to build.

Her army experience taught her that freedom without preparation can become another form of danger. Because of that, she does not simply encourage adventurers to follow their dreams. She makes sure they have food, equipment, routes, backup plans, and somewhere to return to.

Her central belief is:

> People should be free to choose danger, but nobody should be sent into it carelessly.

Her central contradiction is:

> She wants people to live freely, but she is always tempted to protect them by controlling the situation.

## Personality

### Surface traits

- Warm and companionable.
- Lightly teasing.
- Witty without constantly seeking attention.
- Confident and socially comfortable.
- Serious when responsibility is involved.
- More relaxed away from formal work.
- Emotionally sincere but restrained.

### Strengths

- Strong expedition planning and field judgment.
- Understands discipline without treating people like soldiers.
- Good at building trust with adventurers.
- Encourages people without making empty promises.
- Can remain calm when an expedition goes wrong.
- Knows when a situation requires an order rather than a suggestion.
- Makes people feel that their return matters.

### Weaknesses

- Can become overprotective when people ignore preparation.
- Distrusts rigid authority even when structure is necessary.
- Sometimes uses teasing to avoid admitting how worried she is.
- Carries responsibility personally instead of sharing it.
- May confuse giving people freedom with refusing to limit them.
- Has difficulty resting when other people are still in danger.

## Background

Elsie previously served in the army. The details of her service, rank, and reason for leaving should be revealed gradually rather than explained all at once.

Her army experience gave her discipline, planning skills, and a strong understanding of how quickly poor preparation can endanger an entire group.

However, she does not want the Company to become another military institution. She wants its adventurers to have structure without losing their independence.

Her desire for freedom is not abstract. It is connected to something she experienced while serving in the army, though the exact nature of that experience remains undisclosed until the story is ready to reveal it.

## Goals and Motivation

### Immediate goal

Build an expedition system where adventurers can take meaningful work and return safely.

### Long-term direction

Create a Company culture that combines freedom with responsibility. Adventurers should be able to choose their paths, but they should also understand the consequences of poor preparation.

### Emotional need

Learn that protecting people does not require carrying every responsibility alone or controlling every outcome.

## Voice and Dialogue Rules

Elsie's humor comes from light teasing, dry observations, and the contrast between her relaxed personality and her serious professional standards.

She may tease someone because she is comfortable with them. When she becomes genuinely worried, the humor becomes quieter or disappears entirely.

Her speech should be:

- Warm but not overly casual.
- Direct without sounding harsh.
- Confident without sounding imperious.
- Occasionally playful.
- More serious when discussing injuries, preparation, or someone failing to return.
- Free of military jargon unless the scene specifically concerns her past.

Example lines:

> “You can call it a plan after you decide how everyone gets home.”

> “I’m not saying no. I’m saying pack twice.”

> “Good work. Try not to make me praise you twice in one day.”

> “You’re free to take the contract. You’re not free to pretend the risk does not exist.”

Her sincere lines should be simple rather than dramatic:

> “I’m glad you made it back.”

> “You did well out there.”

> “Get some rest. You’ve earned it.”

She should not immediately explain how much she cares. The restraint is part of the character.

## Character-Specific Humor

Elsie's humor should not be constant banter. It works best when:

- She lightly teases someone she trusts.
- She points out the difference between bravery and poor preparation.
- She gives sincere praise and then lightly undercuts the seriousness.
- She remains warm during ordinary Company life.
- Her army discipline clashes with someone else's casual attitude.

Her humor is different from:

- Tristitia's elegant, controlled sass.
- Mae's unhurried matter-of-factness.
- Liliana's uncertainty and overthinking.
- Chasm Elsie's playful gunslinger performance.

## Likes and Dislikes

### Likes

- Capable people.
- Good ale.
- New places.
- Adventurers who return with both their equipment and their good sense.

### Dislikes

- Recklessness disguised as bravery.
- Empty talk.
- Leaving people behind.
- Orders that treat adventurers as expendable.

## Chapter 1 Behavior

Elsie does not need to appear immediately in Chapter 1 unless the story requires her presence.

When introduced, she should:

- Notice how adventurers are being treated.
- Pay attention to whether the Company has preparation standards.
- Respect the Player's idea but question its safety.
- Be more approachable than Tristitia, but more serious than Mae.
- Offer practical advice without taking control of the entire Company.
- Make it clear that an expedition is successful only when people return.

## Relationships

### The Player / Commander

Elsie sees the Player as an outsider with an unusual idea and limited practical experience.

She may tease the Player's lack of knowledge, but she does not treat him as incompetent. She respects that he is willing to build something instead of merely complaining about the existing system.

Her relationship with him should feel companion-like. She may advise him, challenge him, joke with him, and occasionally remind him that leadership means being responsible for people who trusted him.

### Tristitia

Elsie respects Tristitia's field competence but is wary of how naturally Tristitia accepts lethal solutions.

Their tension should not become hostility. Elsie believes Tristitia understands danger very well, but she wants that understanding used to protect adventurers rather than simply eliminate threats.

Tristitia may see Elsie as too concerned with rules. Elsie may see Tristitia as too willing to treat danger as ordinary.

### Steady Mae

Mae's calm gives Elsie room to relax. Mae does not demand that Elsie explain her army past, and Elsie appreciates that.

Mae may quietly point out when Elsie is over-preparing or refusing to rest. Elsie may pretend to ignore her while following the advice anyway.

## Core Feeling

> “You can choose your own road. I’ll help make sure you survive it.”

## Character Tagline

> “Good adventurers aren’t born in comfort—they’re forged by the road, and they grow stronger together.”
