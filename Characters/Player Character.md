# Player Character

Name: VANILLA (Replaceable in-game; “Vanilla” is used in the storyboard for clarity.)

## Character authority

This sheet defines the Player's stable base personality and dialogue model. The Player is not a blank slate. Future scenes should give him authored reactions, observations, and lines that fit this base, while allowing the player to choose his attitude.

The Player's dialogue choices may shape his expression, morality, and relationships, but they should not erase his fixed background, motivations, or ability to form meaningful bonds.

## Role

- Player Character.
- Company Commander.
- Outsider from Earth who becomes the founder and organizer of the Company.

## Core Design

The Player is a lightly authored self-insert, inspired by Vanilla from *Steambot Chronicles*.

He has a recognizable base personality, but the player decides how that personality expresses itself. He can be polite, awkward, playful, demanding, cautious, greedy, generous, sincere, or foolish depending on the selected choices.

The Player should feel like a person the player is inhabiting, not an empty camera and not a completely predetermined protagonist.

The *Steambot Chronicles* influence applies to:

- Intent-based dialogue choices.
- A voiced and authored base character.
- Multiple possible attitudes toward the same situation.
- Player-defined reputation and relationships.
- The ability to be kind, selfish, foolish, or admirable without changing the entire story premise.

It does not apply to the backstory. Unlike Steambot's Vanilla, the IMC Player remembers his life on Earth and does not begin with amnesia.

## Physical Description

- Age: Early 20s.
- Height: Approximately 167 cm.
- Appearance: Young male with short black hair.
- Gender: Male.
- Distinguishing features: Intentionally ordinary so players can project themselves onto him.

## Fixed Base Personality

The Player's default voice should be:

- Curious and reactive.
- Conversational rather than silent.
- Willing to ask obvious questions.
- Sincere enough to state what he is thinking.
- Capable of mild, situational humor.
- Easily overwhelmed by the unfamiliar, but able to adapt.
- More interested in practical answers than dramatic declarations.
- Able to cooperate even when uncertain.

He is not necessarily naive. He simply has no reason to understand Eurydica, monsters, adventurers, or local customs at the beginning.

His humor should often come from honest guesses, awkward timing, or taking strange statements literally.

## Flexible Player Expressions

The player may shape him along several dimensions:

- Friendly or guarded.
- Polite or demanding.
- Cautious or impulsive.
- Earnest or playful.
- Generous or self-interested.
- Patient or impatient.
- Respectful or irreverent.
- Brave, fearful, or strategically careful.

These choices should influence how others respond to him, but he should still feel like the same person underneath.

A rude or greedy choice should not suddenly make him sound like a completely different protagonist. His sentence structure, basic responsiveness, and ability to react to the world should remain recognizable.

## Strengths

- Adaptable in unfamiliar situations.
- Good at seeing possibilities other people overlook.
- Capable of bringing unrelated people around a shared idea.
- Willing to ask questions others consider obvious.
- Can form strong attachments.
- Has enough practical judgment to learn from experience.

## Weaknesses

- Initially lacks knowledge of the new world.
- Can misunderstand danger or social customs.
- May make impulsive guesses when confused.
- Can become dependent on more experienced people early on.
- May prioritize returning home before understanding what staying would cost emotionally.
- His flexible morality can make his intentions difficult for others to predict.

## Background

The Player comes from Earth and suddenly awakens in the outskirts of Eurydica. He remembers his previous life, but nothing prepared him for the existence of another world.

His Earth background should influence his questions and assumptions without turning him into a constant reference machine. He may compare ideas internally, but he should gradually learn to understand Eurydica on its own terms.

## Goals and Motivation

### Immediate goal

Survive in the new world and find food, shelter, safety, and reliable people.

### Long-term goal

Find a way to return home.

### Emotional tension

As the Player builds the Company and forms bonds, returning home may become increasingly complicated. Home remains a genuine desire, but it gradually competes with the life and people he has built in Eurydica.

## Dialogue Rules

- Player dialogue options should describe intent, not exact personality labels.
- The spoken line should remain authored and natural.
- Do not make the Player completely silent or emotionally blank.
- Give him questions, reactions, observations, guesses, and occasional awkward humor.
- Keep his base lines direct and conversational.
- Let the player decide whether he is polite, rude, timid, bold, greedy, or playful.
- Avoid making every option a joke.
- Avoid making every option morally heroic.
- Preserve the feeling that the Player is choosing how to respond, not choosing between unrelated characters.

Examples that fit the base:

> “You told me to.”

> “It’s a… bandit?”

> “Dragon? With a knife.”

> “I’m lost. Could you help me?”

## Relationships

### Tristitia

The first person the Player meets. She is the first source of safety, information, and danger assessment. The Player may respond to her with fear, respect, curiosity, irritation, or humor.

### Steady Mae

The first person the Player meets through Tristitia. Mae is more approachable, but the Player still has to prove that his Company idea can function in practical terms.

## Character Direction

The Player's early arc is not about becoming a different person. It is about discovering what kind of person he will be when given responsibility.

He begins as someone trying to survive another world. He gradually becomes someone other people trust to organize work, protect livelihoods, and make decisions that affect an entire Company.
